# How It Works

The technical half of Project Infinity. For what the game is and how to play it, see the [README](README.md).

Most AI RPGs let the language model make up numbers. Project Infinity runs every dice roll, stat change and combat action through an external game engine the AI can read but not invent.

- **Real Rules, Real Dice** — every attack, save, spell, crit, kill and XP award is resolved by the engine in one call; the GM sees the results and cannot generate them.
- **A Persistent World** — stats, inventory, gold, spell slots, equipped gear and reputation live in a real database; standing is kept per power inside the age it was earned in, and saving records a session timeline so a long campaign stays coherent.
- **What You Wear Matters** — armour class is derived from the equipped set (one suit of armour, two hands, one shield); attunement limits magic items; donning/doffing and hand requirements are enforced; carrying capacity uses the SRD variant encumbrance rules.
- **Spell & Item Effects** — damage resistance, immunity and vulnerability and condition immunity from worn items **and** active spells are applied automatically when the player takes damage; typed active effects (resistances, spell dice, speed, HP-per-level) are tracked and revert cleanly when they end.
- **NPCs With Stat Blocks** — the GM registers each combatant once with HP, AC, per-ability saves, declared attacks and resistances; the engine resolves their attacks, damage-type math and conditions.
- **Multi-Target AoE** — Fireball, Sleep and area spells resolve every target in one call: per-target saves, HP-pool exhaustion, one slot consumed.
- **Conditions** — blinded, grappled, incapacitated, prone, restrained, poisoned, frightened, invisible, paralyzed, petrified, stunned and unconscious drive advantage, forced criticals and failed saves. Exhaustion is tracked as a level 0–6 with its full SRD table (checks, speed, attacks/saves, halved HP maximum, death). A condition a spell applies is written onto the combatant automatically; the GM only removes it when it ends. Every `buffs` field an active spell declares is consumed by the engine — Haste's speed and AC, Foresight's advantages, Mind Blank's psychic immunity, Pass Without Trace's Stealth bonus, Beacon of Hope's saves, Resistance's one-shot die — not stored as a passive note.
- **Refused Actions** — attacking with a weapon not in hand, casting with both hands busy, or casting in non-proficient armour is refused and the action is spent. No do-overs.
- **Rest & Leveling** — short and long rests and XP thresholds auto-apply hit dice, slots, Arcane Recovery, HP, proficiency and progression per SRD 5.1.

Under the hood:

- **The engine is authoritative.** A local **MCP server** with an in-memory SQLite database, initialised from your `.player`. Every action is a verified tool call; the GM's protocol lives in [`GameMaster_MCP.md`](GameMaster_MCP.md), and each engine tool's contract is its own description (surfaced from `dice_server.py`).
- **Dice & checks** — `perform_check` (`d20 + modifier` vs a DC, natural-20/1 criticals) and `roll_dice` (any notation, e.g. `3d6+2`). Player ability/skill checks are engine-derived (ability + skill proficiency, doubled for Expertise or halved for Jack of All Trades, + item bonuses), and passive Perception/Investigation/Insight (10 + modifier) are exposed on the sheet.
- **State authority** — `modify_player_numeric` and `update_player_list` own all state; HP clamps to `[0, max]`; dropping to 0 starts death saves (`make_death_save`), massive leftover damage is instant death, and healing any real HP clears the counters.
- **Exhaustion & concentration** — `modify_exhaustion` tracks the SRD level 0–6 and the engine applies the whole table (checks, speed, attacks/saves, halved HP maximum, death); a long rest removes one level. The engine also tracks the player's concentration spell and rolls the CON save (DC 10 or half the damage) when damage lands, ending the spell on a failure. The sheet's **Condition** card shows the exhaustion level, the active concentration spell and the death-save counters.
- **Combat authority** — `register_combatants` declares a full stat block once (one block can cover **many identical mooks**, listed by `names` — the group shares **one** initiative roll and acts together, while each keeps its own HP); arrivals roll their own initiative and join the order; `resolve_attack` and `resolve_magic` derive AC, saves, damage types and conditions from it; `update_combatant` adjusts the registry mid-fight; `end_combat` closes one the fiction has ended (the engine also ends it on its own once every hostile is killed, fled or surrendered). A declared **companion** is registered by name alone, its sheet filled in by the engine, and its checks and saves are derived from it exactly as the player's are. **The registry is fight state and is emptied the moment a fight ends**, so nothing about a combatant — a companion's hit points above all — can carry into the next one. The client shows a **combat bar** built from that registry — initiative order, HP, conditions, role and status — with an informational turn cursor (the last actor, the **next in line**, the round). The order is **shown, never enforced**: the Game Master decides who acts when.
- **Phased resolution** — the GM resolves all mechanics first (pausing between the two phases on a system handshake), then narrates, so results are mechanically correct before the story is told.

## A turn, mechanically

1. **The mechanics phase.** The GM emits its tool calls in one batch — dice, attacks, spells, state changes. The engine runs each one, and hands back only what the GM does not already hold: the **delta** of a state change, the **exact mechanics** of a roll or attack, and any **error**. Redundant snapshots (inventory, carrying, equipment) are not re-sent; a value the sheet omits is one `read_player_field(fields=[...])` away. The **client keeps the complete, untrimmed result**, shown behind the **Tools** toggle.
2. **The pause handshake.** After a batch the GM emits a pause token and waits. The engine resumes it, and the GM's next response is the narrative. The token is the GM's **obligation, not the engine's requirement**: a GM that narrates straight after a batch still ends the turn — the audit step it was supposed to re-check is simply skipped (`web/tests/live_gm.py` reports that as a failure, and it happens on real turns). Both tokens have one canonical spelling (`{{pause}}` and `{{resume}}`); the engine matches them tolerantly — stray underscores, spaces and case do not break the handshake — and a marker-shaped token it does not recognize is never taken for the turn's prose, so step 5's recovery round still runs.
3. **The narrative phase.** The GM writes the prose. It never writes mechanics, a heading or a placeholder.
4. **The Mechanics panel** is **composed by the engine** from the tool results and appended at the end of the narrative. Every line names who acted on whom (`Actor casts Spell → Target`, `Actor Attack → Target`), shows a green, hoverable **Initiative Order** (tooltip = the order) and each NPC's live sheet as a hover tooltip on their name. While a fight is live, a **combat bar** above the transcript lists the combatants in **initiative order** — name (hover = sheet), ally/enemy, alive/dead/fled, HP, conditions, and the round, with the **next in line** highlighted; when the fight ends the bar closes and the engine adds a one-line tally (`Combat over — 3 dead, 1 fled`) to this block. Turn order is **shown, never enforced**: the Game Master adjudicates the sequence. The GM narrates the outcomes, emphasizes critical results (a crit, near-death HP, a condition), and explains rules when the player needs them.
5. **The engine guarantees the beat.** If a turn reaches the end with no prose — the GM echoed the pause token, attached the illustration alone, or emitted an empty response — the engine takes one non-quiet recovery round and asks for it. The guarantee is driven by whether narrative text actually reached you, never by what the model claimed.

## What the Game Master sees

**The character sheet is engine-injected, and the engine decides what a caster knows.** Each session builds a lean **CHARACTER SHEET** block from the live database — identity, hit points, gold, speed with its encumbrance, conditions, features, the pack by name, what is worn and held, consumables, the era-scoped **reputation** shape to write against, and the **companions** of the party with their stable look — and re-injects it after every Device jump. It carries exactly what the Game Master narrates from and the keys it addresses a write with; the combat math it must never invent (ability scores, saves, skills, proficiencies, AC, hit dice) and the spell lists are absent, because the engine owns them. So a caster may only cast a spell the character has: a cantrip from the cantrip list, a leveled spell that is prepared or known, a **domain / oath / circle** spell (always prepared, and never counted against the number that can be prepared), or a spellbook spell cast as a **ritual**; a scroll carries its own. A miss casts nothing and spends no action — the engine returns the character's real options, and the Game Master offers them.

**A session loads, in order:** the **GM protocol** ([`GameMaster_MCP.md`](GameMaster_MCP.md)); the **ERA INDEX** — the era ladder, one line each with its exact year (the era you are in, and the others it could throw you into); the save's **session memory** — the **STATE page** (the current goal, live hooks, clocks, debts, secrets, canon and unpaid foreshadowing; in the era game it is the page of the age you are standing in) followed by **EVENTS** (the compacted chronicle plus the recent entries kept verbatim); and the **known places** — a `kingdom → area → place path` tree (realm → settlement → district → spot → any nested rooms, any depth) with each place's **main NPCs** as their recorded identity (e.g. *The Drowned Lantern → Common Room · NPCs: Maera — the innkeeper, dwarf rogue; the grandson — the table-runner*). The tree is **scoped to the current era**, **capped at the 12 most recently used places**, and shows each regular's **role, race, class and state** — never a look, and not the place descriptions, which stay in the save. The identity is read BEFORE the GM narrates; it is not echoed in a tool result after the prose. Past the per-place cap (two regulars) or in another area, `lookup('places')` is the uncapped record. With images off the tree comes from the places the GM declares with `note_place` instead of from the illustrator's manifest; the engine keeps the same registry either way. A regular's **state** renders on its place's line — *`Maera — the innkeeper, dwarf rogue (dead)`*. It can come from any source: a combat kill (whoever dealt it), a declared `dead`, or the GM's own word (`petrified`, `gone`). The engine's death index is the one permanent value; it is committed when the age ends or the game is saved, so a reload or a return to that age never resurrects a death, while a heal before that point still saves her. Other states are the fiction's and can change. (Recurring storyline characters live in the on-demand record, not the place tree.)

The character is then pulled live from the database — stats, HP, inventory, equipped gear and the carrying/encumbrance block. The current era's scaffold ([`config/eras/`](config/eras/)) arrives with the awakening, and only **that** era's history and polities are ever in play.

The static prefix — the tool schemas plus the protocol — is the same on every turn, so it is measured and gated (see **Development** below). Nothing about the mode you are not playing is in it.

## The state model

A save is two files in `output/`, plus its images:

| File | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `{name}.player` | The character: abilities, HP, hit dice, inventory and equipped set, spellbook and slots, conditions, gold, and reputation |
| `{name}.timeline` | The session memory the GM writes when you save: the event entries, the **state pages**, the compacted **chronicle** of older events, and the era legends |
| `output/images/{name}/` | Portraits, place seeds and cached illustrations for that save |

**Saving is in place** — a save rewrites its own files and never renames them, because its images are keyed to its name. The engine's database is in-memory and seeded from the `.player` at session start, so the file is the record and the running session is authoritative for the turn in front of you.

**A live session is not the save file.** It is a referee process, the GM's conversation and the place registry, all held in memory; a server restart ends every session. A browser that is still attached keeps its session alive indefinitely, however long the player is silent. Only after the **last** browser detaches does the cleanup clock start: the session is kept for `PI_SESSION_TTL_SECONDS` (default 3600, `0` = never reap) so a dropped socket or a sleeping laptop can reconnect, and is then discarded. A turn already running is allowed to finish (a turn hung past `PI_SESSION_MAX_BUSY_SECONDS`, default 6 hours, is treated as dead). Anything since the last Save is lost when a disconnected session is discarded, which is why the client warns when a session expires.

**One live session per save.** Starting a game on a save that is already open in another live window is refused (`409`); a disconnected session on that save is closed and replaced, so an old, stale copy cannot later save over newer progress. The place registry has the same guard: a closing session only discards the registry whose write ticket it holds.

**Reputation is scoped.** In the era game it is stored per age — `{era: {polity: {faction: [entries]}}}` — so standing earned in Egypt is never offered in Wallachia and never lost when you leave; the GM still writes the short path and the engine resolves the age. In the classic game it is a flat map keyed by kingdom. Each age's memory of you lives in the timeline as a one-line **legend**, written the first time that age sees you vanish.

**The state notebook is scoped too, in the era game.** An age is a perspective, so the timeline holds **one page per age** with the seven fields that belong to that age (goal, hooks, clocks, debts, secrets, canon and unpaid foreshadowing), and only the age the Traveller is standing in is ever injected — the ages you are not in cost nothing. `Standing` is gone: a power's attitude is the engine's own era-scoped **reputation**, a name is the place tree's, and anything left is a hook, a debt or canon. Companions are not written by the Game Master either: the engine lists them in the injected character sheet (see below). A page is rewritten on Save, and an age's page is also rewritten on the **closing turn** as you leave it, because once an age is left no save can ever happen there again. The engine checks a page's field names and order when it is written and again when it is read — it drops what another store already owns (the era, the year, the place and its regulars, reputation, standing, the companions, the Device) and keeps the Game Master's prose untouched — and a page that loses its `Goal` keeps the one already held, or is asked for once. The classic game has no ages and keeps a single notebook. A save made before the pages were scoped has one unkeyed block: the engine reads it as the age the Traveller is in and rewrites it keyed on the next save.

**Every age is dated to one exact year** (`meta.when`, e.g. Wallachia `1460`, Egypt `2560 BC`), shown in the always-on ERA INDEX (`- wallachia (1460) -- …`). A period would let the Game Master drift and would invite the notebook to invent its own year.

**A companion is a combat sheet, not a prompt block.** A companion is a full character marked `ally`, at most three in the party. The Device carries your companions with you, so the party crosses the ages; in the classic game the four kingdoms are one world, so it crosses them too. The Game Master declares the sheet once with `register_companion` — its **ability scores are required**, plus AC, hit points, attacks, `advantages` (combat-only rules like *Sneak Attack 2d6*) and any resistances or immunities, plus the same stable physical look a recurring NPC gets, so the illustrator keeps the face — and from then on registers it in a fight **by name alone**. The engine fills the block from its store and hands the filled line back with the registration; its **name**, identity and look are in the injected **CHARACTER SHEET** (re-injected after every jump) — the sheet itself never enters the prompt as combat data. **No current HP is stored**: a fight states it, full by default, so a rest never has to heal a companion and a wound can never leak into the next fight. Because it has ability scores, the engine also rolls **its own checks and saves** (`perform_check(actor="…")`) from the sheet instead of trusting a number the Game Master computed. At three companions in the party, a fourth is refused until the player decides who leaves.

## Two modes, one engine

The mode is chosen at creation, stored on the save, and hidden from the GM.

| | Classic | Time Traveler |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World | A fixed invented realm — the political scaffold is injected at awakening | A ladder of ages — four historical, plus New York, the Traveller's own — one file each in `config/eras/` |
| Where you start | The one world | **Rolled** from the playable ages — but never New York, the Traveller's own. Later ages are yours to choose |
| Reputation | Flat, per kingdom | Per age, then per polity and faction |
| The Device | — | Yours to command from the first turn: choose the age, and — once you have stood somewhere in it — the place; leave the place and the Game Master chooses where you land. There is no countdown and no forced jump. Its **four parts** are real inventory items and the key to the future — New York is unreachable until every one is held |
| `lookup` tool | Offered — the place record (`places` / `places: AREA`) and a tool's on-demand field reference (`ref:TOOL`) | Offered — any era's scaffold and factions, plus the same `ref:TOOL` references and the place record |

The mode costs nothing when it is not in play: the protocol is marker-gated and rendered per session, and the era index is its own system message. `lookup` is the one generic read-only tool and is offered in **both** games — in classic its era branches simply have no other era to reach, and in both it answers the **place record** (`places`, or `places: AREA`) from the registry the session holds, so a settlement's places can be read on demand instead of inferred from the block.

**Difficulty is orthogonal to the mode.** **Hard** plays strict SRD as written — a lone hero in a party-of-four world. **Easy** keeps exactly the same resolution and adds the balancing guidance the Game Master reads: encounters, tactics, pacing, DCs, and how failure lands. The rules never change; the world does. Like the mode it is marker-gated in the protocol (`<!-- EASY:ON … -->`), so a Hard save pays nothing for it.

## Storyline image continuity

**Images are recommended.** The place-and-NPC tree the GM sees, and the places a Time Traveler may aim at (an area the save has stood in, then up to 12 places inside it), are kept in **both modes** and recorded as soon as the GM declares the place: with images on the scene call itself is the declaration (its picture only adds the seed file), and with images off the GM declares with `note_place`. A place can therefore be **known without a picture** — it is in the registry, but its hidden establishing view has not been drawn, so the GM is still asked for that one-line description; when an image is finally requested, the file is added to the same entry. The block the GM reads is scoped to **where the Traveller stands**: that area's places (up to 12 per area, the same bound the picker uses), the current place marked, and — for every other area the save has stood in — one line naming **one exact place path there to reuse**, so a return is declared with the path the save already holds instead of a fresh invention. It is a **reminder, not the record**, and says so: the header names the on-demand **place record** (`lookup('places')` for every settlement the save holds with a place count each, `lookup('places: AREA')` for one settlement's places or a whole kingdom's), which the engine renders from the same registry — so the block and the answer can never disagree, and the answer is the registry's own spellings. The record is uncapped and carries what the block's per-place cap drops: the regulars past the first two, and every regular in another area, as name, role, race, class and state; its footer names the **place-less recurring people** with the same identity — the cast and the declared recurring characters, which the block never shows and the timeline cannot stand in for (it is prose; this is the identity the engine checks declarations against). Past the anchor cap the block says `+N more areas you have stood in` rather than dropping them silently. The engine also keeps the block's `← you are here` line (and the caption, the arrival and the state attribution) honest: a turn declares **one** place — the one it ends in — and at the end of every **text-mode** turn that did not declare exactly one the engine asks once, quietly: none means a forgotten move or a stale caption; more than one means it must be told which place the turn **ended** in (it keeps a single current place, every declaration arrives before the prose, and any pick between two would be arbitrary — the model has the prose by then). It never reads the prose itself (a move into a room the save has never held cannot be inferred from it), only checks the count. A turn that declares nothing costs one extra model call (a stay turn, under the movement rule); a move the GM declared itself — one place — costs none. The echo asks for the **place alone**, and a declaration that repeats where the story already stands changes nothing and writes nothing — a repeated regular included, verbatim or not — so keeping the block honest never rewrites the save; the engine's teaching notes stay quiet on a repeat too. That registry lives in memory for the **session** and reaches the save only when you save: a session that ends without saving leaves the save's places exactly as they were. You choose the age, then the area and the place once you have stood there; only the new-character start is random. Images add the portrait and the per-turn illustration.

- **A place has an address** — `kingdom → area → place path` (realm → settlement → district → spot → nested rooms, any depth), named once and reused exactly. Declaring a place the story has already been to is how the GM says it **moved back there**: the engine's idea of where you are (the caption, the arrival string, and which place a regular's state belongs to) follows that declaration — or the scene call, with images on, which carries the place every turn. A declared path that drifts — a dropped article (`Western Market` for `the Western Market`), different case or spacing, or a known place nested under a parent the GM invented — is matched forgivingly and **filed under the path the save already holds**, so a return can never duplicate a place into the registry. Once the save holds a place, a declaration may name only it — `the guard room`, with no address — and the engine keeps the saved path, address and spelling. A declaration is a **delta**, never a replacement: it adds or updates the regulars it names and leaves every other one in place, so `{name: "Huiyuan", state: "petrified"}` is a whole update and a re-declaration can never wipe a roster. Each regular may carry a **state** — one short word, the GM's own ("petrified", "gone"): it renders beside the name in the tree and the record, and any source can produce it — a combat kill (whoever dealt it), a declared `dead`, or the player's action narrated into a declaration. The one value the engine consumes is `dead`, which joins the place's permanent death index (a declared death is never cleared, while a word like "petrified" stays the fiction's and can be changed). With images on, where `note_place` is hidden, the **scene call's `main_npcs`** is the state's carrier. A room the save does **not** hold must come with its settlement; a bare name is filed in the settlement the Traveller is in, with a note telling the GM so (a place with no settlement at all is refused rather than filed blind, because the block's scoping reads the area).
- **There are no preset landings** — the age's file carries no arrival list. When you release the Device into an age you have not stood in, the Game Master chooses where the Traveller lands and declares it (`note_place` in text mode; the scene call carries it with images on). Once you have stood somewhere, its **area** joins the picker, and up to **12 places inside it**; each joint is optional — name the age, or an area you have stood in, or the exact place, and whatever you leave out the Game Master chooses (an area alone means *land them somewhere in it*). The arrival string is always the Game Master's declared place, and a resumed save connects it to the place its manifest already holds.
- **The crossing ends your spells** — a Device jump (like a long rest) ends the running spell effects: every buff reverts, a disguise lapses, concentration drops, and the transient conditions clear. A lasting mark stays — a **curse**, a **geas**, a **disease** — and so do the durable conditions **petrified** and a long **poisoned**. A short rest ends none of it.
- **Every place gets a hidden "seed"** — an empty, permanent establishing view, never shown to you.
- **Moments are drawn from the seed, never the previous picture** — the action image reproduces the establishing view with you and the cast, so rooms never rearrange and figures can never duplicate. Only the time of day, the weather and the moment's own damage may differ; nothing absent from the seed is invented. Action images are served once and discarded; the seed is the only scene picture a save keeps.
- **Returning and permanent changes** — a revisited place is drawn from the same seed; a lasting change (it burned down) regenerates it.
- **Names in, looks injected** — each recurring NPC is declared once with a **race**, a **class** and a stable **physical** look (no pose, position or action) and the engine injects the look, so a face never drifts. A place's **main NPCs** are declared as a list; the tree carries their role, race, class and state, so the GM reads the recorded identity before narrating instead of reinventing it (there is no late "on stage" echo). The place-less cast's identity is in `lookup('places')`, not the tree. Every declared NPC states its race and class — an NPC is never left human by default. Recurring characters are declared as they appear; one-off extras need none.
- **Time, weather, and no posing** — the GM declares time and weather; the seed stays timeless and weather-neutral. Everyone is caught in the action, never facing the camera.
- **Disguises** — while an appearance-changing effect (Disguise Self, Alter Self) is active, the scene shows that appearance instead of your portrait.

### The icon library

Sheet icons are a **shared, cross-character library**, cached under `assets/{family}/{kind}/{slug}.png` — one store and one manifest per **image-model family**, and a family never serves another family's file. The sheet's treatment is chosen on the start screen: **cached icons only; no generation**, **cached icons + generate on new ones**, or **text only**.

## Technology

| Component | Technology |
|-----------|------------|
| Language | Python 3.11+ |
| Game engine | MCP (Model Context Protocol) server + in-memory SQLite |
| Web server | FastAPI + Uvicorn, streaming over WebSocket |
| Client | Vanilla JS / HTML / CSS (no build step) |
| Data validation | Pydantic |
| Config | YAML |
| World | Two modes: a fixed invented realm (`config/world.yml`) and a ladder of historical eras (`config/eras/`) |
| Game Master models | Ollama Cloud, Google Gemini *(optional)* |
| Images *(recommended)* | Google Gemini image models, cached per save (portraits, icons, place seeds) |

*Model choice* belongs to the provider, not the engine — the offered list changes with the providers, so it lives in the app. One caveat is ours to state: **Gemini ignores custom sampling** (`temperature`/`top_p`/`top_k` are deprecated and fixed by the model), so the temperature control applies to the Ollama models only.

## Development

```bash
venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements-dev.txt   # tiktoken, once
venv\Scripts\python.exe tools\verify.py
```

`tools/verify.py` is the whole gate in one command and exits non-zero on the first failure. Eight steps, in order:

| Step | What it asserts |
| --- | --- |
| `srd` | `tools/srd_gate.py` — every **name** this repository ships is an SRD 5.1 name |
| `spellstats` | `tools/spell_stats_gate.py` — every spell's **numbers** (level, resolution, save, damage, upcast, school, concentration, ritual, healing) match the frozen SRD 5.1 table |
| `spellcov` | `tools/spell_coverage_gate.py` — every spell, `condition` string and `buffs` field is **classified** (a real mechanic or a named GM-adjudicated tag) |
| `budget` | `tools/token_budget.py --gate` — the static-prefix ceilings in `tools/budgets.yml` |
| `snapshot` | `tools/gm_snapshot.py input` — the GM-input snapshot tool still runs (into a temp dir, so the repo stays clean) |
| `dice` | The dice engine suite, `web/tests/dice/` |
| `web` | Every web suite, `web/tests/test_*.py` (no network, no GPU) |
| `node` | `node --check` on `web/static/app.js` |

`--only srd,budget,dice` runs a subset; `--json` gives a machine-readable summary.

**Testing the GM, live and scripted.** The gate is deterministic: every web suite runs offline, and the server suite drives the app with a **scripted model** ([`web/tests/_scripted.py`](web/tests/_scripted.py)) — it replaces the provider dispatch for the length of the suite, so the transport (the WS pump, the tool loop over the real `dice_server.py`, the slash commands, the server-side place persistence) is asserted without a model, and a real provider call raises. What the Game Master actually *does* with a model is [`web/tests/live_gm.py`](web/tests/live_gm.py), run by hand and deliberately outside the gate (the glob is `test_*.py`): one live session, driven in-process, asserting the **turn contract** (a batch carries no prose, the pause token arrives alone in its own round, one pause per batch and none without one, the resume round is canonical, the engine's corrective rounds never fire, no marker drift, the turn is narrated once), the **place declaration** in both modes (`note_place` and only on movement with images off; one `request_scene_image` per narrative turn, attached to the prose, with images on; the registry's exact saved paths; `establishing` only for a place the registry does not hold), the **place echo** in text mode (every action turn ends with a place declared — in the GM's own round or in the quiet round the engine asks for; a turn that declared several is asked which one it **ended** in; a move narrated over a room the save has never held still lands, with its settlement; whether the GM needed the ask is recorded as a habit), and — model-free — the **place record** (`lookup('places')`, a settlement scope and a kingdom scope are answered by the engine from the registry's own spellings, with the regulars' role/race/class, and an unknown name returns the map). One scenario moves into a settlement's place the block does not show: whether the GM asks for the map first is recorded there as a **habit**, never a failure, which is the calibration the whole file follows. Options: `--mode text|images|both`, `--model` (default `deepseek-v4.1-flash:cloud`), `--only id1,id2`; about a minute per mode; the verdict and the frames land in `output/live_gm/<stamp>_<mode>/`.

**The token budget.** Every turn sends the GM a fixed **static prefix** — the tool schemas plus `GameMaster_MCP.md` — which is the game's largest recurring cost, so each ceiling is a written decision in `tools/budgets.yml` and a breach fails the build. `tools/token_budget.py --gate` runs the budget check alone, and **refuses to run** without `tiktoken` rather than gate on an estimate. `tools/gm_snapshot.py` captures and diffs what the GM is actually sent (`input` / `behavior` / `compare`).

Three levers keep the prefix down. **A declaration the engine already returns is not repeated in an always-on description** — the return teaches it at the moment it matters (a cap is stated by `limit` and by the refusal), so the duplicate would be paid every turn forever for nothing. The long **explanations** — `update_player_list`'s `effects` vocabulary, `resolve_magic`'s resolution rules — live in [`config/tool_refs.yml`](config/tool_refs.yml) and are fetched with `lookup('ref:TOOL')`, paid only on the turns they are actually used; the always-on price is the one-line pointer in the tool description. The same route carries the **place record** (`lookup('places')`): the KNOWN PLACES block names it in its own header (about a dozen tokens, both modes) instead of holding a whole kingdom's map it could never fit — the cap is stated rather than paid for every turn. And the **compacted schema may omit a parameter that is only a duplicate spelling** — `update_player_list`'s flat ability aliases (`set_*` / `*_bonus`) are dropped from the prompt (a curated list in `web/tool_schema.py`) because the visible `effects` field reaches the same capability and the engine normalizes both into one entry; the MCP server still accepts them, so a call that uses the alias still works.

The fourth lever is what the GM gets **back** from a tool. Every GM-offered tool has an explicit projection decision in `web/gm_view.py` — keep only these keys, drop everything but these, or send it whole with a stated reason. An unclassified tool is **not** dumped raw: it still loses the always-drop keys, and a coverage test boots the tool list and **fails the build** for a tool with no decision — that test, not the fallback branch, is what makes the decision mandatory. The always-drop keys (the mechanics disclosure, the state snapshots) are stripped at any depth, because a mechanics tool embeds other tools' results. `tool_results_gm_view` is enforced, so this half is measured too. An **acknowledgement** is the one projection that must say nothing the GM already has: `note_place`, `register_npcs` and `request_scene_image` return only the outcome (`status`, plus `count` where the engine may have taken fewer entries than declared), because the declaration is the GM's own input and each of their `note` strings restated the tool's description — the corrections and refusals still ride their own text. The exception is `register_companion`'s `note`: the only GM-facing carrier of *register the companion by name alone*, pinned by its own test.

**Layout.** `dice_server.py` is the engine and the MCP tools; `web/` is the server, the session, the client and the tests; `forge/` is the character creation and the world generation; `config/` is all rules data; `assets/` is the icon library; `tools/` is the gate.

## SRD 5.1 conformance

All embedded D&D 5e rules data in `config/` is derived from **SRD 5.1**, and three gates plus an engine smoke suite keep it that way — **names, numbers, and vocabulary**.

**Names — [`tools/srd_gate.py`](tools/srd_gate.py).** `tools/srd/srd-5.1.json` is the whitelist: **names only**, with the provenance of every list recorded in it — which lists were fetched from the published SRD, which are the repository's own reviewed values, and which are an explicit decision to allow original content. The gate checks every name in `config/*.yml`, the Forge's own literals, and every committed icon's name against it. It runs first in the gate, so the claim cannot rot. Where SRD 5.1 renames a spell to avoid a protected character name, the **SRD spelling is what ships**, and the alias is recorded rather than used.

**Numbers — [`tools/spell_stats_gate.py`](tools/spell_stats_gate.py).** `tools/srd/spell-stats.json` is a frozen, offline table of all **319** spells (built once by `tools/srd/build_spell_stats.py`), and the gate checks that `config/spells.yml` agrees on level, resolution (roll vs save), save ability and half-on-save, base damage and type, and per-slot scaling — plus school, concentration, ritual and healing. Reviewed `EXEMPT` maps record every deliberate deviation and the source data's own gaps, so the gate reports real drift, not noise.

**Vocabulary and behaviour — [`tools/spell_coverage_gate.py`](tools/spell_coverage_gate.py) + [`web/tests/dice/test_spell_smoke.py`](web/tests/dice/test_spell_smoke.py).** The coverage gate fails when a spell, a `condition` string or a `buffs` field is not classified — a real engine mechanic, or a named GM-adjudicated tag. The smoke suite runs **every** shipped spell through the real engine once, so no spell is silently unresolvable. Together with the dice suite they prove all 319 spells run and that every declared mechanic is accounted for.

**What the gates cannot check:** the prose inside a description, and whatever the running Game Master invents from a general-purpose model's training. That last limitation is real and is disclosed in the README. The line the engine *does* draw — which mechanics it resolves and which it leaves to the GM — is written down in the next section.

See [`tools/srd/README.md`](tools/srd/README.md) for what each list is and what to do when the name gate fails.

## What the engine decides, what the Game Master decides

The engine owns the **mechanical skeleton**: attack rolls, saves, damage, hit points, the thirteen conditions and their generic effects, resistance/immunity/vulnerability, concentration, death saves, exhaustion, upcast scaling, active buffs, recurring saves and HP-threshold instant death. Everything below is the Game Master's call by design — not an accident, and not a gap the code is hiding.

- **Condition effects with no generic rule.** 27 condition tags across 31 spells apply a state the engine cannot resolve on its own, so the GM runs the effect and passes `advantage`/`disadvantage` or applies results. Control and removal (`Banished`, `Bound`, `Trapped`, `Possessed`, `Time Stopped`, `Magic Suppressed`, …), transformation (`Polymorphed`, `Gaseous`, `Levitating`), rider debuffs (`Weakened`, `Slowed`, `Outlined`, `Confused`, `Feebleminded`, `Cursed`, `Diseased`) and meta effects (`Spell Interrupted`, `Teleported`, `Altered Memories`, `Illusory Duplicates`, `Varies by symbol type`). A few of these already run a partial engine rule (Slow, Confusion and Contagion run their repeating save; Ray of Enfeeblement runs its post-hit save) — the GM owns only the condition's own effect.
- **Effects with no engine consumer.** Mind Blank's divination immunity, Haste's extra action, Expeditious Retreat's bonus-action Dash, and Jump's tripled distance. Action economy is not tracked at all.
- **Purely narrative spells.** 143 of the 319 resolve as automatic with no roll, no condition, no buff and no HP-threshold kill — cantrips and utility from Detect Magic to Wish. The engine validates the slot and records the cast; the effect is the fiction. (**Power Word Kill** is not one of these: its 100-HP-threshold instant death is engine-resolved — the target dies outright through the normal kill, HP and CR-XP path, or the spell has no effect. An unknown target HP is refused, never guessed.)
- **Timing.** There is no clock: spell durations (1 minute / 1 hour / 24 hours / until dispelled), the expiry of a concentration, and the action to shake or slap a sleeping creature awake are all the GM's to end with `update_combatant` / `update_player_list`.
- **Action economy and reactions.** Bonus-action casts, reactions (Shield, Counterspell, Hellish Rebuke) and Haste's extra action. The engine resolves the spell, not whether the actor still had the action to spend.
- **Initiative and turns.** The engine rolls the initiative, sorts the order and tracks an informational cursor — the last actor, the **next in line** and the round — which the client's combat bar shows. It does not enforce the sequence: which combatant acts when (a foe that acts out of turn, a turn the GM lets slide) is the Game Master's call.
- **Combat and environment.** Cover (+2 / +5), disadvantage for ranged attacks in melee, the once-per-24-hours long rest, item charges and curses, underwater, mounted, surprise and hiding, and grapple/shove contests.
- **Targeting and interaction.** A charmed or dominated creature's "cannot attack the charmer", illusion disbelief, invisibility against See Invisibility or Truesight, and polymorph or wild-shape stat blocks.

This boundary is **maintained, not merely documented**: [`tools/spell_coverage_gate.py`](tools/spell_coverage_gate.py) fails the build when a new spell, condition string or buffs field is neither a real engine mechanic nor a reviewed, named entry — so the list above cannot silently grow.
